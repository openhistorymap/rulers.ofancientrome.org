# Design context — rulers.ofancientrome.org

A gallery of the rulers of Rome, from Romulus to the fall of the West in 476,
where you walk the line of kings, consuls, and emperors — and, for those whose
lives are well enough recorded, speak with an avatar of them.

Sibling in machinery to the OHM map atlases (`map.ofww1.org`), but this is **not
a map** — it is a **portrait gallery on a spine of time**. The hero is the face
and the line of succession.

## Design Context

### Users
History-curious general readers and classics enthusiasts, browsing out of
curiosity (a museum-at-home, not a research tool). Daytime, unhurried, often on a
phone. They want to *recognise faces*, place rulers in order, follow a dynasty,
and read a crisp life — then occasionally "meet" one.

### Brand personality
Three words: **lapidary, monumental, unhurried.** The voice of a museum of
antiquity — confident, dignified, never kitsch. No faux-Latin gimmicks, no
laurel-wreath clip-art. Dignity over decoration. It should evoke the quiet of a
hall of imperial busts.

### Aesthetic direction — LAPIDARY EPIGRAPHIC (committed)

The page is **an inscription cut into travertine**. Stone is cut, not rounded.

- **Theme**: LIGHT travertine (a daylit stone hall), with a "basalt" dark
  variant (lamplit night gallery) on the toggle. Theme derived from context —
  reading biographies in daylight — not from a default.
- **Type**: display **Marcellus SC** (Roman square-capital inscription letters —
  deliberately chosen over the reflexive Cinzel); body **Cardo** (a serif built
  for classicists). One distinctive display + one refined body.
- **Colour** (OKLCH, neutrals all tinted to one warm stone hue ~78): travertine
  ground, deep-umber ink, **gold-leaf** hairlines. Three **period pigments**,
  earthen and low-chroma, carry meaning — Kingdom = terracotta bronze, Republic =
  red-ochre/minium, Empire = porphyry. Pigment appears in rules, lintels, and
  tags — **never as a border stripe**.
- **Texture of craft**: square cut edges (`--radius: 0`), **recessed niches**
  (inset shadow, never a floating drop shadow), **incised** capitals (a faint
  lower-edge highlight reads as carving), hairline gold rules, wide quiet
  margins, generous negative space.

### Signature moves (what you remember)
- Every ruler is a **bust in an arched stone niche** over a carved label plate,
  stamped with its **Roman-numeral catalogue mark** (№ XXIV).
- The **first ruler of each period is a monumental frontispiece** (double-wide
  niche with the lead biography) — the grid breaks on purpose.
- Section headers are **carved lintels**; the dynasty index reads as an engraved
  table of contents with leader dots.
- The 476 boundary is a **porphyry slab** handing off to `rulers.ofthepast.org`.

### Anti-references / banned
Not a SaaS dashboard, not a rounded-card grid with soft drop shadows, not dark-
mode-with-glowing-accents. Explicitly avoided: side-stripe borders, gradient
text, glassmorphism, reflex fonts (Cinzel/EB Garamond and the wider banned list).

### Design principles
1. **Carve, don't float.** Depth comes from recesses and incision, not shadows.
2. **Stone is cut.** Square edges; the only curve is the Roman arch of a niche.
3. **Pigment is rare and meaningful.** Period colour earns its place; gold is a
   hairline, not a fill.
4. **The face leads.** Portraits are the content; chrome stays quiet.
5. **Inscriptional hierarchy.** Few sizes, high contrast, letter-spaced caps for
   labels — long reading stays in Cardo at a comfortable measure (~68ch).

## House rules

- No build step. Plain HTML/CSS/JS, MapLibre-free. Relative paths only.
- `data/` is machine-owned (harvester output). Never hand-edit it.
- Accessibility: real focus states, `aria` on the drawer/toggles, keyboard
  closable, alt text (the ruler's name) on busts, `prefers-reduced-motion`
  honoured (the staggered carve-in reveal is disabled).
- Portraits/biographies hot-linked from Wikimedia/Wikipedia; keep the credit in
  the detail panel and footer.
